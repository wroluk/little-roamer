# Dish Ridge — region card

The ivory receiver rises above three terracotta shelves on the eastern side of Crown Crater. The dish faces southwest, so its concave surface is visible from the crater connection and its western lookout looks back toward both finished regions.

| Feature | Placement and purpose |
| --- | --- |
| West saddle / reset | (130, -201), 23 m elevation; a broad arrival clearing connects directly to Crown's eastern rim. |
| Switchbacks | Two broad rounded turns climb the southern face from 23 to 52 m. Unequal shelves and wind-cut gullies give the road a substantial landform beneath it. |
| Communications dish | (238, -262), on a 52 m service terrace. A 34 m diameter segmented reflector, deep radial lattice trusses, four braced feed arms, a fork pivot, maintenance catwalk and ladder, and a tripod base form the primary landmark. All large parts have matching collision geometry. |
| Summit circuit | A full driveable loop surrounds the dish; small equipment cabinets and cable covers sit within the service island. |
| West lookout | (161, -284), 54 m elevation, on a broad supporting shoulder with a clear view toward Crown and Habitat Seven. |
| Eastern ledge | A narrower rock-surfaced route loops around the eastern face and reconnects to the summit. The switchbacks remain the broad alternative. |
| South descent | A winding descent reaches (245, -80), ready for the next Glassfall connection. The continuation is development terrain until that region is built. |
| Secondary forms | Split wind-carved stone at the saddle, exposed shelf slabs, three offset relay masts, grounded service equipment, and paired amber route markers. |

## Dish feedback refinement

The antenna follows the industrial structure in the [user’s reference](https://static2.bigstockphoto.com/6/2/2/large1500/226529644.jpg): a substantial framework behind the reflector, a heavy elevation bearing, and human-scale service access. The image is a design reference only; the game uses local geometry.

The orange cabinet lids now sit above their bodies with an overhang and clear seam, removing intersecting/coplanar side faces. Martian rock emits warm terracotta fragments through a Mars-specific particle palette. The camera can tilt farther upward, raising its gaze after reaching a low, ground-safe boom position.

### Tripod connection refinement

All three angled legs extend below the tops of their raised sockets and into the foundation pads. The three rising orange braces now meet a shared dark collar; an ivory vertical support connects that collar to the bearing above. The visible supports and their collision geometry agree. Close views: [west](dish-tripod-review/tripod-west.png), [south](dish-tripod-review/tripod-south.png), [east](dish-tripod-review/tripod-east.png).

### Relay placement refinement

The three smaller structures are communications relay antennas. Their original positions on the steep rear slope produced oversized exposed foundations. They now stand inside the level service island, clear of the summit circuit, on low footings. Segmented antenna panels, thin aerials and small service boxes replace the blank board-and-beacon appearance. The old hillside placements are removed. Review: [relay array](dish-relay-review/relay-array.png), [level foundations](dish-relay-review/relay-footings.png).

### Service access connection

The maintenance deck now has a solid bridge extending into the azimuth bearing housing, with two diagonal supports tied back to the central tripod column. The ladder meets the supported deck. [Side view of the connected access platform](dish-tripod-review/service-bridge.png).

The two deck braces now terminate at the centre of the tripod's shared collar. Their upper ends are embedded in the thickened deck slab, so neither cut end is exposed. The [service bridge side view](dish-tripod-review/service-bridge.png) shows the revised connections.
